The policy, and what changed

RBP Policy v2.0.0, approved by the CVE Board on 13 August 2026.

What it expects

The policy states the expectation plainly:

A CVE Record should be published within 72 hours of either (a) disclosure by the CNA or (b) the CNA becoming aware of a third-party disclosure, as applicable.

and aligns itself to two CNA Operational Rules sections:

  • {{ rule_must }}: CNAs MUST publish within 72 hours of publicly disclosing a CVE ID they assigned. Past that, the CNA's Root may direct a CNA-LR to publish.
  • {{ rule_should }}: CNAs SHOULD publish within 72 hours of becoming aware that a third party disclosed.

Rule 4.5.3.5 is also relevant: CNAs MUST reject unused or unpublished CVE IDs. A long-lived reservation is not a neutral state under the rules.

What enforcement looks like

Four levers, which the Program “may take” and which “may be applied individually or combined”: Warning, Reservation Caps, Intervention, Formal Review. Remediation deadlines are set case by case by a TL-Root or Root.

Parts of it are mandatory, and quoting only the discretionary parts would be selective. The policy says a Root will notify the CNA of the affected IDs and the required remediation timeline; that CNAs MUST prioritise requests from their Root to publish RBPs identified as critical; and that repeated violations may escalate “up to and including CNA decertification”.

So the accurate claim is narrower than “no enforcement”, and it is the one that survives contact: the process exists and is entirely unobservable from outside, so nobody can tell whether it ran. There is no public RBP list, no public record that a CNA was notified, and no public enforcement log.

One factual note for anyone searching: RBP Policy v1.0 is not in force. It was removed from cve.org in January 2022 at the Program Lead's direction pending a new version (cve-website #835), and v2.0.0 is that replacement. Copies of v1.0 are still mirrored by third parties and still rank in search, and its 5% and 50% thresholds should not be cited. This project cited them itself before checking, which is how we know how easy that is.

The field that would let anyone check

Ask the CVE Services API about a published ID and it tells you who assigned it:

GET /api/cve-id/CVE-2026-26100
{"state":"PUBLISHED","owning_cna":"Nozomi"}

Ask about a reserved one and it does not:

GET /api/cve-id/CVE-2026-2574
{"state":"RESERVED","owning_cna":"[REDACTED]"}

The field is populated and served, and withheld for reserved IDs. Being precise about scope: that redaction covers every reserved ID, on the order of tens of thousands for 2026 alone, and the overwhelming majority of those are not RBP at all. The endpoint cannot tell which are, so a blanket rule is the only rule it can apply.

CNA Rule 4.5.1.7 is worth quoting in full rather than in half, because both sentences matter and the second one is the one that binds:

The Secretariat MAY publicly identify the CNA who reserved the CVE ID 24 hours after a CVE ID has been Publicly Disclosed. Otherwise, the Secretariat SHOULD NOT publicly identify the CNA until the CVE Record has been published.

That is a rule about the Secretariat's own conduct. It is not this site's permission to name anyone, and the site does not claim it as one. What it does establish is that the Program already contemplates naming a reserving CNA once an ID has been public for 24 hours. So the ask is narrow and grantable: unblind owning_cna only for reserved IDs already publicly referenced for more than 24 hours. A blanket unblinding would be unsafe, and this project is the reason we can say why: the same block-boundary reasoning this site uses to recover one owner would let anyone derive pre-disclosure ownership across the entire reserved space.

The metric that used to exist

A quarterly Reserved but Public table went live on the CVE Metrics page in February 2021 and was commented out on 7 February 2022. The markup is still in the site source, frozen, its final column already reading N/A.

The documented sequence is more mundane than it first looks, and the record is in the repository this site already links. Issue #835 (25 January 2022) withdrew the v1.0 policy PDF, “Per the Program Lead”, noting it “will be replaced with a new version of the document at a later date”. Thirteen days later issue #842 commented out the RBP table as item 2 of a three-item quarterly-to-annual table restructuring. That reads as routine maintenance, not concealment, and the RBP glossary entry is still live on cve.org today.

Two corrections to earlier versions of this page. The table's public life was closer to four months than a year, because cve.org itself did not launch publicly until September 2021. And the archived series is not what this site publishes: it was a quarterly flow of newly identified RBPs, from 4,326 in Q1 2017 down to 350 in Q3 2021, measured with authoritative internal access. The headline here is a live stock, discovered from feeds that reach a minority of CNAs, with roughly half the owners inferred. The two are not comparable and this site does not replace it.

The replacement promised in 2022 has now arrived as v2.0.0. Our ask is that the series comes back with it.
