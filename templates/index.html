What this site does not tell you

This site does not say which CNA reserved any of these IDs. It can often reconstruct that from the surrounding ID blocks, and it does so internally to measure how well the reconstruction works, but it publishes none of it. The method, its measured error rate, and why the figure is not yet strong enough to publish a name against are all on the method page.

That is a deliberate limit, not a gap waiting to be filled. The argument here is that owning_cna is populated, served, and masked by choice, and that the Program published an RBP metric until 2022 and stopped. Neither claim needs this site to name anyone.

What the policy says in the CNAs' favour

This site quotes the policy's hardest line at the top of this page. The same document carries four passages that cut the other way, and this project's own standard is that quoting only the parts that suit us would be selective. They are reproduced verbatim below, under the section headings they actually occupy, because two of them are not in Timely Publication and filing them there would misdescribe the policy.

Timely Publication

CNAs should ensure timely publication of CVE Records, “recognizing that such publication may, at times, coincide with ongoing vulnerability or incident response activities.”

“The CVE Program recognizes that internal processes may necessitate short delays; however, time flexibility allowed by the CNA Operational Rules should not be used to intentionally delay publication once an advisory or Fix has been made public.”

Notification and Remediation

The CNA is expected to publish RBPs as soon as possible and “no later than the deadline stated by their TL-Root or Root (which may account for factors such as volume, complexity, and resource constraints).”

Enforcement

“The CVE Program may take further action depending on the CNA's volume, history, and severity of RBPs.”

Taking those seriously changes what this site can claim, and it already has. Nothing is listed until it has been public for {{ summary.get('min_age_days', 7) }} days, which is {{ ((summary.get('min_age_days', 7) * 24 / expectation_hours) | round(1)) }}x the {{ expectation_hours }}-hour window and well past any short delay or live incident response. The median row here has been public {{ summary.median_days }} days, and {{ summary.age_buckets.get('180d+', 0) }} have been public more than 180 days. A short delay is not what this list is measuring.

And the last two passages license the sentence this whole site is built around: the only deadline that binds a specific row is one a Root set privately. It accounts for volume and resource constraints, it is not published, and no outside observer can see it. That is precisely why this site measures days public and never calls a single row overdue.

The Program used to publish this

A quarterly Reserved but Public table appeared on the CVE Metrics page in February 2021. It was commented out of the page on 7 February 2022. The markup is still in the site source, frozen, its last column Q3 2021. Its public life was closer to four months than a year: the markup was added in February 2021, but the cve.org site itself did not reach v1.0.0 until September 2021, so most of that year the table existed in source rather than in public.

The record shows routine maintenance rather than concealment, and all three exculpatory facts belong here rather than one click away. The table was commented out as item 2 of a three-item release, so it was not singled out: issue #842 lists item 1 as updating the Published Records and Reserved IDs tables with Q4 2021 stats and item 3 as converting the CNA-versus-CNA-LR table from quarterly to annual totals. The v1.0 policy PDF had been withdrawn thirteen days earlier pending a replacement. And the final column already read N/A, so the series had stopped being populated before anyone commented it out.

The replacement has now arrived as v2.0.0. The series has not. This site does not replace that series and is not comparable to it: the archived table was a quarterly flow of newly identified RBPs measured with authoritative internal access, and the count above is a live stock discovered from feeds that reach a minority of CNAs, with roughly half the owners inferred. RBP Policy v2.0.0 names "Program metrics and audits" as one of its own identification channels; our ask is for the public face of that channel. The full sequence, with the issue numbers.
